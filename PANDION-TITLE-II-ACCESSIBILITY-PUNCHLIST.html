Pandion Plots Accessibility and Instructional-Use Punch List

Issue-by-issue remediation plan for product-wide accessibility and Title II readiness when Pandion is required by a public institution.

Audit snapshot and scope clarification: July 28, 2026 · Production DOM and responsive checks + local source review · No product code changed

Scope and compliance baseline

Pandion is an independent personal product with no government affiliation. Its public website does not become a government website merely because its creator separately works for a public college. This checklist does not claim that the website or product is itself a Title II public entity.

When a public college requires students to use Pandion, however, the college may be making the tool available as part of its educational program. The institution's required access path can include course links, download and installation pages, instructions, the application workflow, generated results, and support. The institution's legal and accessibility offices determine the exact boundary, deadline, approval, and accommodation process. See the DOJ Title II web rule fact sheet and implementation guidance.

Pandion's professional product target should be WCAG 2.2 Level A and AA. For covered public-institution use, the current federal technical minimum is WCAG 2.1 Level A and AA, unless the institution applies a stricter standard. The issue findings below remain relevant under either track.

  • Always in the product-quality audit: the standalone application, shared chart engine, essential documentation, exports, and jamovi integration.
  • In the institutional-use audit: every page and workflow students must use to obtain, learn, operate, and receive support for Pandion.
  • Marketing-only pages: retained here as professional accessibility work, but not labeled Title II requirements solely because they are part of Pandion's independent public website.

This is an engineering audit and tracking tool, not legal advice or a certification of conformance.

What is already organized and working

  • Marketing pages have unique titles, lang="en", coherent heading order, primary landmarks, and descriptive image alternatives.
  • No duplicate IDs, missing image alt attributes, positive tab order, or visible unnamed controls were found on the marketing pages scanned.
  • Default text contrast passed on Home, Gallery, Downloads, About, Support, and 404; the confirmed contrast failures are concentrated in the user guide.
  • Home, Gallery, Downloads, and Support produced no page-level horizontal overflow at a 320 CSS-pixel viewport.
  • The shared chart SVG is focusable, uses role="img", and exposes a useful chart summary that directs users to the Statistics panel for numeric detail.
  • The chart engine includes live announcements, visible focus styling, chart-part keyboard navigation, reduced-motion handling, and an existing axe-based regression gate.
  • Standalone modal shells are labelled, set aria-modal="true", hide the application background from assistive technology, and generally restore focus.
  • Data role assignment has a click/keyboard picker, so dragging is not the only way to place variables.
